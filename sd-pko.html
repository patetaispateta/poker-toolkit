Short Deck Fundamentals Triton RulesPKO
Before anything else — the rules that make short deck genuinely different from regular hold'em.
Triton hand rankings (flush beats full house):
Royal Flush > Straight Flush > Four of a Kind > Flush > Full House > Straight > Three of a Kind > Two Pair > One Pair > High Card.
This is critical — full houses are relatively common in short deck. Flopping a full house does NOT mean you're safe.
The deck — what's removed
Cards 2–5 are removed. The deck runs 6–A across all four suits = 36 cards total. Aces still play high AND low (A-6-7-8-9 is the lowest straight — the wheel). No 2–5 means no 5-high straights, no wheel draws the traditional way.
Ante structure you're playing
Button posts ante + blind. All other 5 players post an ante. This means there are 6 antes in the pot before a card is dealt. The pot is always worth fighting for — this structure rewards aggression and position even more than regular hold'em.
How equity changes — the most important concepts
Pairs are stronger preflop

With 36 cards, pairs connect with the board far more often. Set frequency is dramatically higher — roughly 1 in 5.5 vs 1 in 8.5 in regular hold'em. Small pairs gain value.

Suited hands are weaker preflop

Only 9 cards per suit remain. Flush draws complete less frequently and have fewer outs. A flopped flush draw in SD is not the powerhouse it is in regular hold'em.

Straight draws are more powerful

Cards are more bunched — connected hands make straights more often. Hands like J-9, T-8, 9-7 gain significant value. Open-ended straight draws often have 8 clean outs.

Overcards are weaker

With fewer cards, A-K vs a pair is closer to a coin flip than in regular hold'em (roughly 45–55% vs pair). Don't massively overvalue unpaired big cards.

Full houses are more common

Because paired boards happen more often, full houses are NOT the nuts they feel like. Under Triton rules a flush beats them — adjust your stack-off threshold.

Ace plays both ways

A-6-7-8-9 is the nut low straight (the "wheel"). Ace-low straights are possible. A6 suited picks up both a nut flush draw AND a wheel draw on certain boards — genuinely premium.

BTN 6-max SD (~65%+ range, 30BB+)
Open essentially everything. In SD with antes, the BTN is a licence to steal on almost every hand.
The only folds: The weakest offsuit unconnected hands like K6o, Q6o, J6o — and even these are borderline opens vs weak blinds.

Push / Fold Short DeckPKO
SD push/fold ranges are wider than regular hold'em. Suited connectors and pairs gain value; pure overcards lose some. The bounty overlay widens calling ranges further.
Key SD push/fold difference: In regular hold'em you'd hesitate to shove T9o at 12BB from CO. In short deck it's a standard shove — T9 has massive straight equity and nearly 45% vs many calling ranges. Connected hands shove earlier and wider in SD than intuition tells you.

SD PKO specific spots to watch
Set over set hunting

In SD, set-over-set is more common. When stacking a bounty player, be aware that your set may not be the best hand. A flush or a higher set beats you. Don't auto-stack off a low set on a flush-possible board for a bounty.

Flush vs full house bounty shoves

Under Triton rules a flush beats a full house. Don't call off a large bounty shove with a full house on a flush-possible board without thinking about whether they have the flush. This is a common SD PKO leak.

3-way pots and bounties

In SD 3-way all-ins are even more chaotic — equity runs closer. Overcall wider in 3-way bounty pots — you only need ~25% equity to profit when two bounties are at stake and pot odds are favourable.

Don't forget ICM near bubble

SD's higher variance + PKO bounty hunger near the bubble is a dangerous combination. ICM cost of busting outweighs a bounty in most near-bubble spots. Tighten call-offs even in SD.

Postflop Shifts Short Deck
How to adjust your postflop thinking in short deck. The removed cards change board textures, draw strengths, and made hand values in ways that catch intuition players off guard.
Board texture changes
Paired boards are common

With 36 cards, paired boards appear far more frequently. Full house potential is everywhere. Don't assume your top pair or two pair is safe — someone flopping a set and improving is routine in SD.

Flush draws are weaker

Only 9 cards per suit. A flopped flush draw has 7 outs instead of 9 (one on board, you hold two = 6 remaining, but other suited cards may be in play). Don't overvalue flush draws — they're not the semi-bluff monster they are in regular hold'em.

Straight draws are stronger

Connected boards are more common. Open-ended straight draws have 8 outs just like regular hold'em, but they complete more often because the deck is bunched. Gutshots are more valuable in SD — respect them.

Two pair is not safe

In regular hold'em two pair is a strong hand. In SD, two pair is frequently behind sets (which appear on ~18% of flops vs any pocket pair) and is beaten by straights, flushes, and full houses constantly. Play two pair carefully — it's a medium-strength hand in SD.

Top pair needs caution

Top pair top kicker is strong but opponents will have two pair, sets, and straights far more often in SD than regular hold'em. Don't pay off three streets with TPTK without a read. Stack-off threshold is higher.

Nut flush is still the nuts (almost)

Under Triton rules flush beats full house. Nut flush draw is your most powerful semi-bluff in SD. When you have the nut flush draw, you beat not just bluffs but all full houses if you get there. Defend and semi-bluff aggressively with nut flush draws.

Practical postflop rules of thumb
Nut flush draw = stack off

In Triton SD, the nut flush draw beats full houses if it gets there. Play it like a set in regular hold'em — semi-bluff hard, call raises, even consider stacking off as a draw.

Full house ≠ auto stack-off

Always check if a flush is possible before committing your stack with a full house. If the board has three of a suit and your opponent is shoving, they may well have the flush.

C-bet frequency down

Because boards connect so well with SD ranges, c-betting wide as a bluff is less effective. Opponents will have pairs, draws, and made hands more often. C-bet for value more, bluff less.

Implied odds are massive

When you have sets or nut draws in SD, implied odds are enormous — opponents will stack off with two pair and non-nut flushes regularly. Call more preflop and on the flop with nutted draws.

Bet sizing — go bigger

In SD, draws complete so often that slow-playing is punished more. Bet larger with your strong made hands — 75–100% pot is common and correct. Don't give cheap cards.

PKO postflop note

Against a large bounty player, loosen your stack-off threshold slightly — the bounty EV compensates for marginal postflop calls. But don't call three streets of value with two pair just for a bounty.

Key Reminders Short DeckPKO
The things that catch intuition players out most often in SD PKO. Read before every session.
The biggest SD intuition traps
Full house ≠ near-nuts

Your regular hold'em brain screams "stack off" with a full house. In Triton SD it loses to a flush. Always check for flush possibility before committing your stack.

Overcards aren't big favourites

AK vs 88 is basically a coin flip in SD. Don't 4-bet shove AK expecting to be a 60% favourite — you're not. Recalibrate your equity assumptions for all overcard vs pair matchups.

Suited hands lose preflop value

Your regular hold'em instinct adds equity to suited hands for flush potential. In SD that bonus is smaller — only 7 remaining flush outs. Don't over-inflate suited hand values preflop.

Connectors gain preflop value

The opposite is true for connected hands. T9s, J9s, 98s are genuinely powerful in SD — open them from early position, call 3-bets with them, give them credit postflop.

Small pairs are premium

Set frequency of ~18% means 66, 77, 88 are strong opening hands from any position. Don't treat them as speculative in SD — they're genuine value hands.

Nut flush draw = power move

In Triton rules the nut flush draw beats full houses when it gets there. Semi-bluff aggressively, call raises, stack off as a draw when the price is right. It's the most undervalued draw in SD.

PKO reminders in SD context
Call shoves wider — double reason

You call wider in SD (equity) AND wider for bounties. Combine both adjustments — don't just apply one. A medium bounty + SD equity can flip clear folds into clear calls.

Don't stack off for a bounty on a flush board

Classic leak: call off a bounty shove with a full house on a three-flush board. The bounty doesn't compensate for being drawing dead. Check the board first.

Variance is extreme in SD PKO

Short deck is already high variance. PKO adds another layer. Expect wild swings. 10-tournament losing runs are normal. Don't adjust strategy based on short-term results.

ICM still matters near bubble

The excitement of SD PKO can make you gamble near the bubble. ICM cost of busting outweighs bounty EV in most near-bubble spots — same principle as regular PKO.

Ante structure — stay aware
Pot is always worth stealing

6 antes means a full pot before a card is dealt. Folding your button or SB without a fight is a significant mistake in this structure. Attack blinds relentlessly from position.

BTN paying ante+blind

You're paying more than anyone else on the BTN. This is not a reason to be passive — it's a reason to be more aggressive and protect your investment by stealing the antes you paid.

Stack depth shrinks faster

6 antes per hand means stacks erode quicker than in blind-only games. Recalculate your BB count every orbit — you hit push/fold territory faster than you expect.
