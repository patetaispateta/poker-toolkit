BTN 8-max PKO (~45%)
Open nearly everything. Position + bounty opportunity on blinds = wide range justified.
PKO bonus: If SB or BB is a short stack with a large bounty, lean toward the wider end of BTN opens — you want to be the one putting pressure on them.
Sizing: 2.2x. Small enough to steal light, big enough to deny easy calls.
SB 8-max PKO — vs BB only
Open range ~50%+: All pairs, all Ax, all Kx suited, Qx/Jx/Tx suited, all offsuit broadways, K8o+, Q9o+
Never limp SB: Raise or fold. Always.
PKO note: If BB has a big bounty, open even wider and be more inclined to barrel when they fold equity is high. If BB has NO bounty, tighten slightly — pure chip EV game.
Sizing: 3x to maximise fold equity and make BB's defence uncomfortable.
vs 3-Bet Defence 8-Max PKO
PKO changes 3-bet dynamics in both directions. Opponents 3-bet wider to attack your bounty. You should 3-bet less as a bluff vs players who will call wide for your bounty.
Key PKO shift: Opponents will call your 4-bets and shoves wider than vanilla because your bounty overlay makes it profitable. This means your 4-bet bluff frequency should come down — lean toward value 4-bets more than in vanilla MTTs. Conversely, when YOU are 3-betting, widen your value range slightly because you'll get called lighter.

PKO Scenarios 8-Max PKO
Common PKO spots that trip players up and how to handle them correctly.
Spots where PKO changes the correct play
Short stack shoves into you
Their bounty adds real $ to your call. Use the calculator — a marginal fold in vanilla (K9o, 55) may be a clear call once bounty equity is added. The bigger their bounty relative to buy-in, the wider you call.
YOU are the short stack
Opponents call your shoves wider because they want your bounty. Tighten your shove range slightly vs callers who are likely bounty hunting. Avoid marginal shoves (K8o, Q9s) that would be fine in vanilla.
3-way all-in for a bounty
If there's a short stack all-in and another player calls, overcall wider than normal — you're not just competing for chips, you're competing for the bounty. Even a small equity share justifies calling.
Big stack opens, you're in blinds
If the big stack has a large accumulated bounty on their head, 3-bet more aggressively as a bluff — fold equity is valuable and winning the pot doesn't get them the bounty. But if called, be prepared to play cautiously OOP.
Two all-ins ahead of you
If both players are all-in before you, one of them is getting eliminated regardless. You have a chance to win a bounty for free by calling with a wide range — even a coin flip is fine here given the dead bounty equity.
Near the bubble
ICM pressure increases near pay jumps. Do not over-call for bounties when ICM cost is high. A bounty worth $5 is not worth busting out of a spot worth $50 in ICM equity. Tighten call-offs near the bubble.
Your own bounty grows large
When you've knocked out several players, your own bounty becomes a target. Expect wider calls and 3-bets against you. Reduce bluff frequency, tighten marginal spots, and avoid putting your stack at risk in thin spots.
Don't over-prioritise bounties
A common PKO leak: calling off too wide because "there's a bounty." Bounties add EV — they don't flip losing calls into winning ones. Still need reasonable equity. A 25% equity call is still a losing call even with a bounty overlay.

PKO Reminders 8-Max PKO
Mental checklist for PKO-specific discipline.
Core PKO principles
Bounty = extra EV, not a reason to call

Bounties add to your EV on profitable calls. They do not create EV on losing calls. Still need equity.

You collect 50%, not 100%

Half the bounty goes in your pocket. Half goes on your head. Only factor the 50% you keep into call math.

Your bounty makes you a target

The more you accumulate, the wider opponents play vs you. Reduce bluff frequency as your bounty grows.

Isolate bounty-rich short stacks

3-bet or re-isolate to get heads-up with short stacks carrying large bounties. Don't let the table limp-call them.

When PKO math matters most
Mid-to-late stages

Early game bounties are small. PKO math kicks in hard once stacks are 20–30BB and bounties have grown.

Short stack shoves

This is your primary bounty spot. Recalculate pot odds every time a short stack shoves — include the bounty.

Bubble awareness

Don't chase bounties near the bubble. ICM value of staying alive trumps a small bounty in most spots.

Final table dynamics

At the final table bounties can be massive. Recalibrate every level — the game changes dramatically as bounties stack up.

Common PKO leaks to avoid
Calling too wide for bounties

Most common leak. A bounty does not justify a 25% equity call. Run the math before making big calls.

Bluffing bounty hunters

Players gunning for your bounty won't fold. Stop bluffing them and start value-betting thinner instead.

Ignoring ICM near the bubble

ICM and bounties pull in opposite directions near pay jumps. Bounty hunting near the bubble costs tournament equity.

Forgetting your stack depth

PKO call math assumes you can survive the call. Calling off your whole stack for a small bounty at a deep ICM spot is usually wrong.
